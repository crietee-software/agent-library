# Documents

Brandbook 01 v1.1, pages 6 and 16. For onderzoek, offerte, rapport, advies, build plan, and a review file. An invoice uses the visual system and the voice; it does not grow a table of contents.

## Language

Dutch, unless the user asked for English. Skill chat may stay English. The file the client receives follows this page.

First mention: **Crietee Software**. After that: **Crietee**. Never `CRIETEE`, `CreeTee`, `Crieteé`, `CrieteeSoft`.

In the logo: cut-C + `rietee`. In running text: Crietee.

Address the reader as `je`, unless the client prefers `u`.

## Voice

Clear first. An ordinary sentence beats a clever one.

Short. One idea per sentence. Two sentences beat one clause.

Concrete. Say `webshop in twee weken`, not `oplossingen op maat`.

Dutch words over English when a Dutch word exists. A needed term gets one explanation the first time.

Active. Name who does the work.

Friendly and direct. Dry is fine. Rude is not.

Slogans (`Software met een snede.`, `Scherpe software. Zichtbaar vooruit.`) stay on the website, social, and a presentation cover. A document title says what the piece is about. No pun, no riddle, no kicker-as-title.

## Shape

After the letterhead, the first prose is a summary: the conclusion in plain words.

| Rule | When |
|---|---|
| Table of contents at the front | More than five pages |
| Numbered chapters whose titles say what is inside | Reports, memos, plans, quotes with sections |
| Each improvement point: how it is now, what to do, why it helps | Reviews and advice |
| Source on every figure, clickable in the source list | Research and any number in a memo |
| Title ends with a period | Every file |

## Before send

1. Every title names the subject.
2. Someone without the jargon gets it on the first read.
3. Tone is short and concrete, no cliché, no riddle.
4. Name is spelled as Crietee in text, cut-C + rietee in the logo.
5. At most one gold accent per surface.
6. Contrast is ink on paper or paper on ink.
7. The logo has its clear space.
